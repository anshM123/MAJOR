To the Editors of Physical Review Letters

Submission: "A Higher-Spin Gap Constrains the Shear and Hall Spectral Weight of Three-Dimensional Conformal Field Theories", by Ansh Mishra and Aryan Senthilkumar

Dear Editors,

We submit the enclosed Letter, with its Supplemental Material, for publication in Physical Review Letters.

Strongly coupled quantum matter with a gravitational dual is expected to share the transport of Einstein gravity, most famously the ratio η/s = 1/4π. How close a given theory must come to that behavior, and what measurable quantity records the deviation, has not been made quantitative. The Letter answers both questions for conformal field theories in three spacetime dimensions, where the stress tensor's three-point function has two non-Einstein coefficients: the parity-even t₄ and the parity-odd α_T, which exists only in three dimensions.

First, we derive a Hall-channel counterpart of the known shear sum rule. Together the two sum rules equate the thermal spectral weight of the stress tensor in the shear and Hall channels, measured from its Einstein value, to (t₄ + iα_T)/32. We check this exactly in free field theories, in large-N Chern–Simons–matter theories at every coupling, and on the AdS₄ black brane. Combined with the conformal-collider bound, it places every theory that meets the sum-rule conditions in a disc of radius 1/8, with free fields and Chern–Simons–matter theories on its edge and Einstein gravity at its center.

Second, in theories with a large central charge, a gap Δ_g in the higher-spin spectrum shrinks this disc. Without any positivity assumption the radius is about 0.56/(Δ_g − 2), so a thermal spectral deviation δ requires a higher-spin state below twist 2.25 + 5/(9δ): a thermal measurement bounds the microscopic spectrum, with no gravitational calculation. We state openly that this 1/Δ_g scaling is three powers weaker than the parametric expectation. At Δ_g = 1000 we certify, by a computer-assisted proof in interval arithmetic, that a dispersive functional is positive on every heavy state, which reduces the radius to 9 × 10⁻¹⁰ in the planar limit; the step from that positivity to the bound uses an estimate of the light states that we only sketch, and the Letter says so.

Third, in the dual black brane the parity-odd cubic coupling produces a Hall viscosity, 4π|η_H|/s = (3/40)|α_T|, without the axion or magnetic field that earlier holographic constructions required. The neutral case had not been studied systematically. The same bound then constrains shear and Hall viscosity together, and a combination of viscosity and spectral weight in which the cubic coupling cancels gives a corrected lower bound on η/s that tracks the known violations of 1/4π.

We believe the Letter meets the criteria of Physical Review Letters because it connects a vacuum property of a conformal field theory, the non-Einstein part of its stress-tensor three-point function, to finite-temperature observables in both the dissipative and the non-dissipative channel, with explicit constants. The results are relevant to holography and quantum gravity, the conformal bootstrap, and the transport of strongly coupled quantum matter, including parity-violating fluids in two spatial dimensions. We have written the main text for that readership and put the technical detail in the Supplemental Material.

The Letter marks each result as proved, derived, certified or sketched, and states its assumptions in one place; the Supplemental Material grades every statement on that scale. The transport results use the bulk effective theory, and the Letter says which pieces are conditional. A derivation of η/s = 1/4π from conformal field theory alone remains open; the Supplemental Material records what such a derivation would require. The functional weights, the positivity certificates with their inputs and outputs, and the code that reproduces every constant are available at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24571131.
