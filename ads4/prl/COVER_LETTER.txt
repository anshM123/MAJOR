To the Editors of Physical Review Letters

Submission: "How Close to Einstein Must Holographic Gravity Be?", by Ansh Mishra and Aryan Senthilkumar

Dear Editors,

We submit the enclosed Letter, with its Supplemental Material, for publication in Physical Review Letters.

Holography predicts that a conformal field theory with a large central charge and a large gap in its higher-spin spectrum is described by Einstein gravity, but so far only as a scaling law: causality forces the non-Einstein graviton couplings to vanish as powers of the gap, with no explicit constants. Our Letter supplies the numbers for three-dimensional theories. Assuming a higher-spin gap Δ_g ≥ 1000, weak coupling below it, and positivity of fixed sum-rule functionals on heavy states, the stress-tensor three-point function matches Einstein gravity to one part in 10⁸ at leading order in 1/c_T: √(t₄² + α_T²) ≤ 2.89 × 10⁻⁸, or |ĝ₃|Λ⁴ ≤ 120 in units of the gap, including the parity-odd coupling that exists only in three dimensions. Most of the factor 10⁸ relative to the conformal-collider bound is the Λ⁻⁴ scaling already implied by causality; what is new is the coefficient. A direct physical consequence: the energy flux created by the stress tensor is isotropic to one part in 10⁸ (anisotropy at most 7.2 × 10⁻⁹, where the conformal-collider bound allows 1). We also bound the first stringy (R⁴) correction from above, under two further stated conditions; ABJM theory, whose R⁴ coefficient is known from localisation, sits at 2.6% of that bound, a consistency check.

The method uses causality in its sharpest CFT form: energy detectors (light-transformed stress tensors) commute at spacelike separation, which gives sum rules on the four-point function ⟨TTTT⟩. The bounds follow once fixed linear functionals are positive on every heavy state. We prove this positivity in the flat-space limit, where it is certified by computer-assisted interval arithmetic. In anti-de Sitter space we establish it with error-controlled numerics on the most constraining states, and at large spin we prove it given a stated decay assumption and model estimates of a few magnitudes. Two new ingredients make the AdS problem tractable: a dual tensor structure in which curvature causes no helicity mixing at leading Regge order, and "de-shifted" weights for which every heavy state contributes its flat-space value at that order. The Letter states its assumptions once and does not hide which parts are proved, which are certified, and which are numerical or conditional; the Supplemental Material grades every statement against a stated legend.

Every number is reproducible. The functional weights are published as exact rationals and tables, separate code reproduces every constant from them, and an independent check rebuilt the key reductions from the written statements alone. Data and code are available at [URL].

We believe the Letter suits Physical Review Letters because it turns a qualitative expectation that is central to holography into explicit inequalities: to our knowledge, the first bounds with explicit constants on the graviton cubic couplings and on an R⁴ coefficient derived from ⟨TTTT⟩ in AdS₄/CFT₃. It should interest readers in holography and quantum gravity, string theory (the R⁴ coefficient and the ABJM check), the conformal bootstrap and analytic S-matrix programmes (dispersive and light-ray sum rules), and the study of causality and positivity constraints on gravitational effective field theories and energy correlators. The main text is written for this broad readership; the technical detail is in the Supplemental Material.
