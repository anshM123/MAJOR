To the Editors of Physical Review Letters

Submission: "How Close to Einstein Must Holographic Gravity Be?", by Ansh Mishra and Aryan Senthilkumar

Dear Editors,

We submit the enclosed Letter, with its Supplemental Material, for publication in Physical Review Letters.

Holography predicts that a conformal field theory with a large central charge and a large gap in its higher-spin spectrum is described by Einstein gravity, but so far only as a scaling law: causality forces the non-Einstein graviton couplings to vanish as powers of the gap, with no explicit constants. Our Letter supplies the numbers for three-dimensional theories. Assuming a higher-spin gap Δ_g ≥ 1000, weak coupling below it, and positivity of fixed sum-rule functionals on heavy states, the stress-tensor three-point function matches Einstein gravity to one part in 10⁸ at leading order in 1/c_T: √(t₄² + α_T²) ≤ 2.89 × 10⁻⁸, or |ĝ₃|Λ⁴ ≤ 120 in units of the gap, including the parity-odd coupling that exists only in three dimensions. Most of the factor 10⁸ relative to the conformal-collider bound is the Λ⁻⁴ scaling already implied by causality; what is new is the coefficient. A direct physical consequence: the energy flux created by the stress tensor is isotropic to one part in 10⁸ (anisotropy at most 7.2 × 10⁻⁹, where the conformal-collider bound allows 1). We also bound the first stringy (R⁴) correction from above, under two further stated conditions; ABJM theory, whose R⁴ coefficient is known from localisation, sits at 2.6% of that bound, a consistency check.

The method uses causality in its sharpest CFT form: energy detectors (light-transformed stress tensors) commute at spacelike separation, which gives sum rules on the four-point function ⟨TTTT⟩. The bounds follow once fixed linear functionals are positive on every heavy state. We prove this positivity in the flat-space limit, certified by computer-assisted interval arithmetic. In anti-de Sitter space we prove, again with certified bounds and independently reproduced, that the leading Regge part of the cubic functional keeps one fifth of its flat-space margin on every heavy state at every gap above 300. The full action is established with error-controlled numerics on the most constraining states, and at large spin under a stated decay assumption. Two new ingredients make the AdS problem tractable: a dual tensor structure in which curvature causes no helicity mixing at leading Regge order, and "de-shifted" weights for which every heavy state contributes its flat-space value at that order. In bulk language the cubic bound reads |ĝ₃|Λ⁴ ≤ 120: defining a scale by |ĝ₃| = M_*⁻⁴, whatever generates the R³ coupling cannot set in below about 0.3 of the gap. The Letter states its assumptions once and does not hide which parts are proved, which are certified, and which are numerical or conditional; the Supplemental Material grades every statement against a stated legend.

Every number is reproducible. The functional weights are published as exact rationals and tables, separate code reproduces every constant from them, and an independent check rebuilt the key reductions from the written statements alone. Data and code are available at [URL].

Why Physical Review Letters. That a large gap forces Einstein gravity is one of the load-bearing beams of holography, and until now it has been a statement about exponents. This Letter turns it into a number. Under assumptions we state once, in any three-dimensional holographic CFT with a higher-spin gap of 1000 the graviton's cubic couplings deviate from Einstein gravity by at most 2.9 × 10⁻⁸; equivalently, the energy flux of the stress tensor is isotropic to one part in 10⁸, where causality alone allows order one. The first stringy correction is capped too, with ABJM theory inside the cap. The positivity that drives such bounds has long been the soft spot of bootstrap arguments in anti-de Sitter space. Here it is proved at leading Regge order, with a computer-certified margin of 20% on every heavy state at every gap above 300, and independently reproduced. Separately, we show that the algebra of energy detectors alone, meaning conservation, conformal symmetry and causality, gives a two-percent bound at every spin, reducing what remains to a single light-ray identity. The results answer, with numbers, how much of Einstein gravity quantum mechanics and causality force on a holographic theory. That question matters across holography and quantum gravity, the conformal and S-matrix bootstraps, string theory, and the fast-growing study of energy correlators. The main text is written for that broad readership; the technical detail is in the Supplemental Material.
