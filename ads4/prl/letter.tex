\begin{abstract}
Holography predicts that quantum field theories with a large gap behave like Einstein gravity, but
only as a scaling law, with no numbers. We supply the numbers for three-dimensional theories: with a
gap of 1000 or more, the graviton's three-point couplings match Einstein gravity to one part in
$10^8$ at leading order, and the first stringy correction is bounded from above, with ABJM theory
inside the bound. The key input is causality, that energy detectors commute, together with a
positivity property we prove in flat space and check numerically in anti-de Sitter space.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\paragraph*{Introduction.}
A conformal field theory (CFT) with a large central charge $c_T$ and a large gap in its
spectrum of single-trace operators is expected to be dual to weakly coupled Einstein
gravity, with higher-derivative corrections suppressed by the gap~\cite{HPPS}. Turning
this expectation into inequalities with numbers tests holography from the CFT side and
constrains gravitational effective field theories in anti-de Sitter space. For the graviton cubic couplings the expectation has been made parametric:
causality of high-energy scattering~\cite{CEMZ} and of CFT correlators in the Regge
limit~\cite{HJK,AHKT,CHP,MeltzerPerlmutter,AKT} forces the non-Einstein three-point
couplings of the stress tensor to vanish as powers of $1/\Delta_g$, without explicit
constants. Averaged null energy is positive~\cite{HofmanMaldacena,HKT}, and the light-ray
operators that measure it commute at spacelike separation~\cite{KKSDZ,KKSDZlightray}; this
gives superconvergence sum rules on $\langle TTTT\rangle$ that force those couplings to
vanish at infinite gap~\cite{KKSDZ,BHM}.

Explicit constants exist in two neighbouring settings. In flat space, dispersive
sum rules bound graviton couplings sharply in terms of the mass $M$ of the lightest
higher-spin state~\cite{CaronHuotVanDuong,CHMRSswamp,BKZ,CHLPS,CHLPSpw}; Caron-Huot, Li,
Parra-Martinez and Simmons-Duffin (CHLPS) remark that their four-dimensional bound
translates to holographic CFT$_3$ (without a derivation)~\cite{CHLPS}, and record
explicit bounds on holographic central charges in higher dimensions~\cite{CHLPSpw}. In
AdS, dispersive CFT sum rules~\cite{CHMRSdisp} gave Caron-Huot, Mazáč, Rastelli and
Simmons-Duffin (CHMRS) explicit-constant bounds on scalar contact couplings, with an
infrared $\log\Delta_g$~\cite{CHMRS} (see also Ref.~\cite{Kundu}). Chang, Landau and
Simmons-Duffin (CLS-D) built the spinning sum rules on $\langle TTTT\rangle$ and listed
the steps still missing before graviton bounds can be derived in AdS~\cite{CLSD}.

We carry out those steps for AdS$_4$/CFT$_3$. Our main result bounds the parity-even and
parity-odd deviations $t_4$, $\alpha_T$ of $\langle TTT\rangle$ from its Einstein value with an
explicit constant; a second result bounds one $R^4$ graviton coefficient from above. To our
knowledge neither has been derived before from $\langle TTTT\rangle$. Both follow from
positivity of fixed linear functionals on heavy states. For the cubic bound we prove this positivity
in the flat-space limit, establish it numerically in AdS on the most constraining states, and prove
it at large spin given a decay assumption and model estimates (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}). We work at
$\Delta_g=1000$ and extend the evidence to larger gaps under a no-growth assumption.
Throughout, ``we prove'' means a written proof, ``certified'' a computer-assisted proof in
interval arithmetic, ``with error control'' a computation in which every modelled term carries
a threefold safety factor (an estimate, not a bound), and ``in a model'' a computation whose
accuracy is itself a hypothesis; the Supplemental Material~\cite{SM} grades every statement
against this legend (Sec.~S1).

\paragraph*{Setting and assumptions.}
The CFT is unitary, parity-invariant or not, with a unique stress tensor $T$, normalised so that
$c_T=1$ for a free real scalar~\cite{OsbornPetkou}; then $8\pi G=256/c_T$ in units of the AdS
radius. \emph{Heavy} states are all states with $\Delta-J\ge\Delta_g$, single- or multi-trace. We
measure the gap by $\Lambda:=\sqrt{(\Delta_g-2)(\Delta_g-1)}\simeq\Delta_g-\tfrac32$ (not a
cosmological constant), and a heavy state $(\Delta,J)$ has bulk mass $m$, with
$m^2=(\Delta-J-2)(\Delta+J-1)$. Our results rest on three assumptions.
(i)~\emph{A higher-spin gap}: every single-trace operator of
spin $J\ge4$ in the $T\times T$ operator product expansion (OPE) has twist $\Delta-J\ge\Delta_g$;
single traces of spin $0$ and $2$ may be light, spins 1 and 3 do not appear in
$T\times T$~\cite{DKKPS}, and odd $J\ge5$ couple only through a parity-odd structure.
(ii)~\emph{Weak coupling below the gap}:
$c_T\to\infty$ at fixed $\Delta_g$, and the OPE data of states with $\Delta-J<\Delta_g$ (other
than light single traces) are those of a tree-level bulk effective theory up to $o(1/c_T)$,
uniformly in spin; all bounds hold at leading order in $1/c_T$.
(iii)~\emph{Positivity}: the fixed functionals defined below are positive on every heavy state.
For the functional of the cubic bound we prove this in the flat-space limit; in AdS we establish
it with error control on the most constraining near-gap states, and at large spin, on sampled
near-gap states and at large impact parameter, we prove it given a uniform decay of heavy-state
contributions beyond the Regge regime and model estimates of a few magnitudes; elsewhere the
evidence is computed in models. The evidence for the quartic functional is described below.
The quartic bound requires in addition the sign $\kappa>0$ of one light-side
coefficient (or $t_4=\alpha_T=0$) and, for its headline value, that no scalar lighter than
$0.46\Lambda$ couples to $TT$.

$\langle TTT\rangle$ is fixed by $c_T$ and two numbers~\cite{HofmanMaldacena,Buchel,CDPrakash}:
$t_4$, and $\alpha_T$, which multiplies a parity-odd structure that exists only in three
dimensions~\cite{GPY}. We write $\tau:=\sqrt{t_4^2+\alpha_T^2}$. In the bulk $\tau=240|\hat g_3|$, with
$\hat g_3$ the complex $R^3$ coupling of Ref.~\cite{CHLPS}; we derive this dictionary and check it
numerically for $t_4$, and derive it for $\alpha_T$, whose sign convention is immaterial since
only the modulus enters. The quartic coupling $g_4$ is defined by the flat-space amplitude
$M(1^+2^-3^-4^+)=\langle23\rangle^4[14]^4f$, $f=8\pi G/(stu)+\dots+g_4+g_5t+\dots$~\cite{CHLPS},
normalised so that type~II strings have $g_4M^6/8\pi G=2\zeta(3)$, $M^2=4/\alpha'$~\cite{CHMRSswamp}.

\paragraph*{Why it matters.}
Under (i)--(iii), every holographic CFT$_3$ with a
gap $\Delta_g=\DgHead$ in the single traces of $T\times T$ has stress-tensor three-point couplings
within $\tau\le\CubicHead$ of their Einstein values. In physical terms, a state created by the stress
tensor deposits energy at null infinity with angular profile $(E/2\pi)[1+A\cos(4\theta-\varphi)]$, where
$A\le\tau/4$~\cite{CDPrakash}. Conformal-collider bounds require $A\le1$ in every unitary
CFT$_3$~\cite{HofmanMaldacena,CDPrakash}, and a free scalar or a free fermion saturates this. Here
$A\le7.2\times10^{-9}$: the energy flux is isotropic to one part in $10^8$, and $\langle TTT\rangle$ is that
of equal numbers $n_s$, $n_f$ of free real scalars and free Majorana fermions, to $|n_s-n_f|\le7.2\times10^{-9}c_T$.
Most of the factor $10^8$ between the two bounds is
the $\Lambda^{-4}$ scaling already implied by causality~\cite{CEMZ,AKT}; what is new is the
coefficient. In units of the gap the cubic graviton coupling obeys
$|\hat g_3|\Lambda^4\le\GthreeHead$, a number rather than a scaling law.

\paragraph*{Mechanism.}
Light-transformed stress tensors~\cite{KSDlight}, which are energy detectors at null infinity, commute when
spacelike separated. Integrating the vacuum expectation value of their double commutator
against a weight gives a sum rule~\cite{KKSDZ,CLSD}. If the weight decays fast enough in the
Regge limit (high energy at fixed momentum transfer), the total vanishes: its Regge spin $k$
must exceed the Regge intercept $J_0$ of the exact correlator, and $J_0\le1$ in every unitary
CFT~\cite{KKSDZ,CaronHuotInversion,PSZ}. Like Refs.~\cite{CHMRS,CLSD}, we use the sum rule at finite
$c_T$, expand only its light part in $1/c_T$, and use the heavy part only through its sign; the
planar intercept, $J_0=2$ for graviton exchange~\cite{KKSDZ}, never enters. Expanding in the
$T\times T$ OPE splits the sum rule into a light part (the identity, $T$ and its multi-traces
below the gap) and heavy states,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\rm heavy}{\rm Tr}\big[P_{\mathcal O}\,M_\Omega(\mathcal O)\big]=-\,\Omega[\text{light}],
\label{eq:sumrule}
\end{equation}
where $P_{\mathcal O}\succeq0$ collects the squared OPE coefficients and $M_\Omega(\mathcal O)$ is
the functional $\Omega$ acting on the blocks of $\mathcal O$. At leading order the light side is
fixed by the bulk couplings: graviton exchange, $|\hat g_3|^2$ and, at $k=4$, $g_4$. If $\Omega$
is \emph{positive} on every heavy state, then $\Omega[\text{light}]\le0$ at every $c_T$, and its
leading term in $1/c_T$ is the bound. In the flat limit $\Lambda\to\infty$ the heavy side becomes
the partial-wave expansion of graviton scattering, and positivity becomes the positivity used
in flat space~\cite{CHLPS}. The difficulty in AdS is that curvature moves each heavy state's
contribution away from its flat value, by amounts that are not small near the gap or at large
impact parameter.

Three ingredients make it tractable. First, a dual tensor structure, $EE$, for which we prove that
AdS curvature produces no helicity mixing at leading Regge order; with the standard structure
the mixing makes the functional negative at large impact parameter in a model calculation. Second,
\emph{de-shifting}: we choose the weights so that, at leading Regge order, every heavy state
contributes its flat value, to about one part in $10^6$ (a generalised Abel equation), leaving only
beyond-leading-Regge corrections for positivity to survive. Third, the light side at $k=4$.
There the $R^4$ contact, which grows like the fourth power of the energy, sits exactly at the
convergence border of the sum rule and contributes a finite Regge ``arc''. We normalise it by
calibrating against a heavy scalar whose exchange produces an $R^4$ contact, and derive
$-\mathcal A_4[R^4]=2g_4$.

\paragraph*{Functionals.}
The functional $\Omega^{EE}$ of the cubic bound combines CLS-D's functional at Regge spins
$k=2,3$ with polynomial weights fixed by a linear program, the $EE$ structure and de-shifting.
The functional $\Omega^Q_{\rm up}$ of the quartic bound adds de-shifted $k=4,5$ pieces. All
weights are published (at $\Delta_g=2000$ and $5000$ as tables), and their de-shift identity is certified for the
load-bearing $k=4$ pieces~\cite{SM}. We derive that the OPE sum may be exchanged with the
integrals, with absolute convergence, whenever $J_0<\tfrac32$ ($k=2,3,5$) or $J_0<\tfrac52$
($k=4$); since $J_0\le1$, this holds at every finite $c_T$ with room to spare. The bounds are
then the $c_T\to\infty$ limit of an exact inequality whose heavy side is non-negative at every
$c_T$ and whose light side is $O(1/c_T)$.

On the light side, $\Omega^{EE}$ sees the graviton pole $16\pi G/(\nu^2+9/4)$ at celestial
frequency $\nu$ and a term $K|\hat g_3|^2$ with $K=1+O(\Lambda^{-2})$. We derive that no term linear
in $t_4$ or $\alpha_T$ survives: at $k=2$ the weight of the sum rule is affine in the light-ray
times, so the stress-tensor exchange enters only through the energy flux and its first moment;
both are helicity-diagonal for Einstein gravity and helicity-flipping at linear order in $t_4$ and
$\alpha_T$, and the $EE$ structure annihilates helicity flips. A direct numerical evaluation gives
zero to $10^{-11}$ of the Einstein term. For $\Omega^Q_{\rm up}$ the $R^4$
arc is an integral over celestial angles, and an analytic counting lemma shows that it
converges; a direct numerical scan follows the predicted trend. We identify $\tilde g_4:=-\frac12\mathcal A_4[\text{light}]$ with $g_4$ up to
$O(\Lambda^{-2})$ by assuming, as CHMRS and CLS-D do~\cite{CHMRS,CLSD}, that light
double-trace data are those of a tree-level bulk effective theory.

Light single traces of spin 0 and 2, allowed by (i), enter the $k=2,3$ sum rules like heavy
states, with a contribution that, by a step we sketch, is fixed by their Regge behaviour up to
an AdS mass shift. We prove that $\Omega^{EE}$ is positive on them at every
mass in the flat limit, so they enter with the
favourable sign and the cubic bound also bounds their couplings~\cite{CHLPS}. By contrast, $\Omega^Q_{\rm up}$ is negative on scalars lighter than
$0.46\Lambda$, which couple to $TT$ through $\phi W^2$ (cf.\ Ref.~\cite{CMT}). This is the origin
of the scalar condition in the quartic bound.

\paragraph*{Result.}
\emph{Assume (i) with no light single trace in $T\times T$, (ii), (iii) for the functional used,
and the analytic inputs listed in the Supplemental Material~\cite{SM}.
Then, at leading order in $1/c_T$:}

\emph{(1) Cubic.} With $L_A=\int_0^1\psi_2(p)\,dp/(p^2+\tfrac{9}{4\Lambda^2})$, where $\psi_2$ is
the published $k=2$ weight,
\begin{equation}
\tau\le\tau_0:=\frac{240}{\Lambda^4}\sqrt{\frac{16L_A}{K}}.
\label{eq:cubic}
\end{equation}
\emph{At $\Delta_g=1000$ and $K=1$,
$\tau_0=2.89\times10^{-8}$, that is $|\hat g_3|\Lambda^4\le120$~\cite{SM}.}

\emph{(2a) Quartic. If either $\tau=0$ or a light-side coefficient $\kappa$ is positive,}
\begin{equation}
\frac{\tilde g_4\Lambda^6}{8\pi G}\le17.2407\ln\frac{2\Lambda}{3}+B_{\rm eff}(\Lambda),
\label{eq:quartic}
\end{equation}
\emph{where $B_{\rm eff}(\Lambda)\approx-19.3$ varies slowly with the gap~\cite{SM}. At
$\Delta_g=1000$ the right side is $\QuartHead$.}

Light single traces of spin 0 and 2 extend the result through the sketched step above:
given it and positivity on them, (1) holds unchanged, and so does (2a) if no scalar lighter than
$0.46\Lambda$ couples to $TT$. \emph{(2b)} Without that condition, the combination
$\Omega^Q_{\rm up}+0.04\,\Omega^{EE}$, which needs positivity of both functionals, gives the
weaker upper bound $\QuartRep$ at $\Delta_g=1000$.

Item (2a) uses only
$\Omega^Q_{\rm up}$, so it does not need positivity of $\Omega^{EE}$; a scalar lighter than
$0.46\Lambda$ does not invalidate it, but adds a term proportional to its squared
coupling~\cite{SM}. The cubic implication (1) is derived; in the quartic bound the subleading
$g_5$ term, the repair in (2b) and, when $\tau\ne0$, the sign of $\kappa$ are sketched, the last
with a model input. Separate code reproduces every constant, and a separate
check rebuilt the $EE$-kernel reduction and the counting lemma from the written statements alone.
A theory with a larger gap also has a gap of $1000$, so these numbers bound it too.
Positivity at its own gap gives a sharper bound (Fig.~\ref{fig:bounds}), $\tau_0\propto
\sqrt{\ln\Lambda}/\Lambda^4$, although in gap units the cubic constant grows slowly, to
$|\hat g_3|\Lambda^4\le128$ at $\Delta_g=2000$ and $139$ at $5000$. The quartic bound is quoted at
$\Delta_g=1000$, where positivity of $\Omega^Q_{\rm up}$ was examined.

\paragraph*{Is the functional positive?}
Figure~\ref{fig:coverage} maps the evidence for $\Omega^{EE}$ at $\Delta_g=1000$. Two statements
are certified: positivity on every heavy state in the flat-space limit (for $\Omega^Q_{\rm up}$
too, outside two asymptotic corners where it is computed), and, in AdS, positivity of the
leading Regge part of $\Omega^{EE}$ on every state whose impact parameter exceeds about a third of
the AdS radius. With error control, $\Omega^{EE}$ is positive on its most constraining state, spin
5 at the gap, near the gap on 25 of the 31 spins up to 32 that have near-gap states (all of them up
to 21 and the odd spins from 23 to 31), and on heavier states at small impact parameter; the
masses covered and the margins are in the Supplemental Material~\cite{SM}. With error control,
$\Omega^Q_{\rm up}$ is positive on its binding states near the gap and at masses $1.3$--$3\Lambda$.
Elsewhere the values are computed in models or interpolated. No computed state is negative; the states examined while the functionals
were being chosen are marked in the Supplemental Material. \HLJstatusLetter

\paragraph*{Large spin.}
At large spin curvature bites hardest: the margin of the functional shrinks as the impact
parameter grows. An exact identity, which we prove through eighth order in
the distance from the Regge limit, check numerically to fortieth order and derive at all orders,
turns every curvature correction into one that the de-shifted weights already absorb, plus one
explicit term. Computed exactly on near-gap states with spins up to $344$, that term cancels and
the corrections use less than half of the margin. With a contour argument for the higher orders,
this gives two conditional theorems: positivity on 47 sampled near-gap states of spin
$26\le J\le344$, and on states of spin below $10^6$ whose impact parameter exceeds about a third of
the AdS radius (remainder checked on sampled states up to mass $5\Lambda$). Both assume, as stated
in (iii), that beyond the Regge regime the difference between a heavy state's contribution and its
flat value splits into two analytic waves that decay at a uniform rate; this holds to all orders of
the saddle-point expansion. The first theorem also uses a bound on one edge integral of the
graviton, estimated in a model and met by an exactly computable scalar analogue; the other
magnitudes the theorems use are model estimates with large margins~\cite{SM}. Between the
samples, and at intermediate impact parameter, positivity rests on a hypothesis supported by a
sketched argument.

\paragraph*{Larger gaps.}
Above $\Delta_g=1000$ the evidence for $\Omega^{EE}$ has at most the strength it has at $1000$ and
uses further premises~\cite{SM}. The certified statements hold at every gap, the leading-Regge ones
wherever the de-shifted weight exists (we construct it up to $5000$). At $\Delta_g=2000$ we
repeated the error-controlled near-gap checks for twenty spins up to $29$: no state is negative,
the checks close for every computed spin up to $21$, for several with a signed estimate of the truncation
error, and up to spin 16 the error budgets shrink like $\Delta_g^{-2}$, as the approach to flat
space predicts.

\paragraph*{A check with ABJM theory.}
In the type-IIA 't~Hooft regime of ABJM theory~\cite{ABJM} the higher-spin gap is the string
scale, $\Lambda\leftrightarrow ML$ at leading order in $1/(ML)$: supergravity fields have spin at
most 2, and the lightest single trace of spin 4 or more is a string state at the first massive
level. So assumptions (i) and (ii) hold at leading order, by an argument we sketch. Through the
orders computed, supergravity and $R^4$, only the stress-tensor multiplet is exchanged in
$T\times T$ at tree level~\cite{CPY,BCP}, so the scalar condition of (2a) holds too, again at the
level of a sketch. The flat limit then gives $g_4M^6/8\pi G=2\zeta(3)$~\cite{CHMRSswamp,BCP},
$2.6\%$ of our upper bound at $\Delta_g=1000$; the identification $\Lambda\leftrightarrow ML$ shifts
this by a few percent. This is a
consistency check at the one-digit level, not evidence for positivity.

\paragraph*{Discussion.}
Under (i)--(iii), the cubic and quartic graviton couplings of a holographic CFT$_3$ with a
large gap in the single traces of $T\times T$ are, in units of the gap, at most of order a hundred
(the quartic one if $\kappa>0$ or $\tau=0$), as expected when the gap sets the size of
higher-derivative terms. The cubic bound is about ten times weaker in
$|\hat g_3|$ than the flat-space optimum transplanted to AdS~\cite{CHLPS} (Fig.~\ref{fig:bounds}),
which uses forward and helicity-flip sum rules not available here, and the quartic bound is
dominated by the infrared logarithm. Beyond the parametric statements of
Refs.~\cite{KKSDZ,BHM,AKT} and the flat-space and higher-dimensional
precedents~\cite{CHLPS,CHLPSpw}, the new elements are explicit constants derived from
$\langle TTTT\rangle$ in three dimensions, the parity-odd coupling $\alpha_T$, and an AdS bound
on a graviton $R^4$ coefficient (which localisation determines in ABJ(M)
theory~\cite{BCP,BCJP}).
