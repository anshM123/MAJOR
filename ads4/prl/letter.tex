\title{How Close to Einstein Must Holographic Gravity Be?}

\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
Holography predicts that CFTs with large central charge and a large gap behave like Einstein
gravity, with corrections that fall as powers of the gap. For three-dimensional CFTs we determine the
constants. A gap of 1000 forces the graviton's three-point couplings to agree with their Einstein values
to one part in $10^8$ at leading order in $1/c_T$. The bound rests on a positivity property, which we establish by computer-assisted proofs in flat space and, at that gap, on every heavy state in AdS.
Under a further positivity assumption we also bound the first stringy correction. Without any positivity input, commutativity of energy detectors and crossing still bound the deviation by $0.018$. Read in
reverse, the bounds locate the string scale: a deviation $\tau$ requires a higher-spin state below twist about $2+17.8/\tau$.
\end{abstract}

\maketitle

\textit{Introduction}---A conformal field theory (CFT) with a large central charge $c_T$ and a large gap
$\Delta_g$ in its spectrum of single-trace operators is expected to be dual to weakly coupled Einstein
gravity, with higher-derivative corrections suppressed by the gap~\cite{HPPS}. Causality of high-energy scattering~\cite{CEMZ} and of CFT correlators in the
Regge limit~\cite{HJK,AHKT,CHP,MeltzerPerlmutter,AKT} forces the non-Einstein couplings of three stress
tensors to vanish as powers of $1/\Delta_g$. Averaged null energy is positive~\cite{HofmanMaldacena,HKT},
and the light-ray operators that measure it commute at spacelike separation~\cite{KKSDZ,KKSDZlightray}; the
resulting sum rules on $\langle TTTT\rangle$ force those couplings to vanish at infinite
gap~\cite{KKSDZ,BHM}. These arguments do not fix the constant in front, that is, how far from Einstein
gravity a theory with a given gap can be. The constant matters beyond AdS/CFT. It turns the idea that quantum
gravity with a large gap reduces to Einstein gravity into a quantitative test. It also limits which gravitational
effective field theories can have a holographic completion, and how large a non-Einstein coupling a holographic
model with a given gap can have.

In flat space, dispersive sum rules bound graviton couplings in units of the mass of the lightest higher-spin state~\cite{CaronHuotVanDuong,CHMRSswamp,BKZ,CHLPS,CHLPSpw}, and
Caron-Huot, Li, Parra-Martinez, and Simmons-Duffin remark that their four-dimensional bound translates to
holographic CFT$_3$, without a derivation~\cite{CHLPS}. In anti-de Sitter (AdS) space, dispersive CFT sum
rules~\cite{CHMRSdisp} have given bounds with explicit constants on scalar contact
couplings~\cite{CHMRS,Kundu}, and Chang, Landau, and Simmons-Duffin built the spinning sum rules on
$\langle TTTT\rangle$ and listed the steps still missing before graviton couplings can be bounded in
AdS~\cite{CLSD}.

Here we take those steps for AdS$_4$/CFT$_3$. We show that a gap of 1000 forces the three-point function
of the stress tensor to agree with its Einstein form to one part in $10^8$ [Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}(a)], and we bound the leading stringy
($R^4$) correction to graviton scattering from above. Both bounds follow from positivity of fixed
linear functionals on the heavy spectrum [Fig.~\ref{fig:concept}(b)]. For the cubic bound we establish this positivity at $\Delta_g=1000$,
on every heavy state, with a computer-assisted proof (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}). Without any positivity input, the algebra of energy detectors together with crossing still gives a weaker bound, valid at
any gap. Read in reverse, it bounds the string scale of any such theory from above in terms of its measured deviation from Einstein gravity. We say ``we prove'' for a written proof and ``certified'' for a computer-assisted proof in
interval or directed-rounding arithmetic. ``With error control'' marks a computation in which every modeled term
carries a threefold safety factor, ``in a model'' one whose accuracy is itself a hypothesis, and ``we sketch'' an
argument whose details are not complete. The Supplemental Material~\cite{SM} grades every
statement on this scale.

\textit{Setup}---We consider a unitary CFT$_3$, parity invariant or not, with a unique stress tensor $T$
normalized so that $c_T=1$ for a free real scalar~\cite{OsbornPetkou}. Its three-point function
$\langle TTT\rangle$ is fixed by $c_T$ and two numbers~\cite{HofmanMaldacena,Buchel,CDPrakash}: $t_4$,
and a parity-odd coefficient $\alpha_T$ that exists only in three dimensions~\cite{GPY}. Both vanish for
Einstein gravity, and we write $\tau=(t_4^2+\alpha_T^2)^{1/2}$ for the deviation. In the bulk
$\tau=240|\hat g_3|$, where $\hat g_3$ is the $R^3$ coupling of Ref.~\cite{CHLPS}. The quartic coupling
$g_4$ is the coefficient of the $R^4$ contact term in graviton scattering, normalized so that type~II
strings give $g_4M^6/8\pi G=2\zeta(3)$ with $M^2=4/\alpha'$~\cite{CHMRSswamp,CHLPS}. We measure the gap
by $\Lambda=[(\Delta_g-2)(\Delta_g-1)]^{1/2}$, which plays the role of the bulk mass at the gap; \emph{heavy} states
are all states, single or multi-trace, with twist at least $\Delta_g$.

We make three assumptions. (i) \emph{A higher-spin gap}: every single-trace operator of spin four or more
in the operator product expansion (OPE) $T\times T$ has twist at least $\Delta_g$; single traces of spin
zero and two may be light. (ii) \emph{Weak coupling below the gap}: $c_T\to\infty$ at fixed $\Delta_g$,
and the states below the gap are described by a tree-level bulk effective theory, uniformly in spin. All
bounds hold at leading order in $1/c_T$. (iii) \emph{Positivity}: the functionals introduced below are
positive on every heavy state. For the cubic functional at $\Delta_g=1000$ we establish (iii) by a computer-assisted proof, given the detector identities used for Eq.~\eqref{eq:angular}, so there it is not a separate assumption. The quartic bound also needs either $\tau=0$ or a positive sign for one light-side coefficient;
the value in Eq.~\eqref{eq:quartic} further needs that no scalar lighter than $0.46\Lambda$ couples to $TT$. The
standard inputs are listed and graded in the Supplemental Material~\cite{SM}. The strongest, used only to identify
the coefficient the quartic sum rule measures with $g_4$, is that the light double-trace data are those of a
tree-level bulk theory, with no fine-tuned cancellation between the heavy states and the tower of bulk contact terms.

\textit{How close to Einstein}---Under (i) and (ii), and with no light single trace in $T\times T$, every holographic CFT$_3$ with a gap
$\Delta_g\ge\DgHead$ in the single traces of $T\times T$ has
\begin{equation}
\tau\le\CubicHead
\label{eq:cubic}
\end{equation}
at leading order in $1/c_T$ [certified arithmetic gives $2.8914\times10^{-8}$]. In units of the gap this
reads $|\hat g_3|\Lambda^4\le\GthreeHead$. If we define a scale by $|\hat g_3|=M_*^{-4}$, then
$M_*\ge0.30\,\Lambda$, so, if its coefficient is of order one, whatever generates the $R^3$ coupling cannot set in below $0.30$ of the gap. The bound also covers parity-violating theories.

Physically, a state created by the stress tensor deposits energy at null
infinity with an angular profile proportional to $1+A\cos(4\theta-\varphi)$, with $A\le\tau/4$~\cite{CDPrakash}.
The conformal-collider bound, which holds in every unitary CFT$_3$, allows $A$ up to one, and a free scalar
or a free fermion saturates it~\cite{HofmanMaldacena,CDPrakash}. Here $A\le7.2\times10^{-9}$, so the energy
flux is isotropic to one part in $10^8$. Equivalently, the parity-even part of $\langle TTT\rangle$ is that
of equal numbers of free real scalars and free Majorana fermions, up to $7.2\times10^{-9}c_T$. Most of the
factor $10^8$ between the two bounds is the $\Lambda^{-4}$ scaling already implied by
causality~\cite{CEMZ,AKT}; our result is its coefficient [Fig.~\ref{fig:bounds}(a)].

A theory with a larger gap also has a gap of 1000, so Eq.~\eqref{eq:cubic} bounds it too. Positivity at its
own gap would give the tighter bounds on $\tau$ of Fig.~\ref{fig:bounds}, with $|\hat g_3|\Lambda^4\le128$ at
$\Delta_g=2000$ and $139$ at $5000$. We have not certified positivity at those gaps. The evidence for it
there rests on further premises, including that near-gap corrections do not grow with the gap, and it does not cover one
band of near-gap spins~\cite{SM}.

\textit{The stringy correction}---If the quartic functional is positive on every heavy state, and either
$\tau=0$ or the light-side coefficient mentioned above is positive, we find at $\Delta_g=1000$
\begin{equation}
\tilde g_4\Lambda^6/8\pi G\le\QuartHead
\label{eq:quartic}
\end{equation}
when no scalar lighter than $0.46\Lambda$ couples to $TT$, and $\QuartRep$ otherwise; the second value uses
positivity of both functionals. Here $\tilde g_4$ is
the coefficient the sum rule measures; it equals $g_4$ up to corrections of order $\Lambda^{-2}$ if the
light double-trace data are those of a tree-level bulk theory, as assumed in Refs.~\cite{CHMRS,CLSD}. The
bound grows only logarithmically with the gap [Fig.~\ref{fig:bounds}(b)]. The subleading terms and the bound without the
scalar condition rest on steps we sketch. So does the sign of the light-side coefficient when $\tau\ne0$, which also
uses a model input.

\textit{Without positivity}---Commutativity of an energy detector with its first moment in retarded time
gives a second sum rule. In this sum rule each heavy family enters through a ladder of helicity amplitudes fixed
by the special conformal Ward identity. An exact telescoping identity makes every heavy contribution non-negative,
and reflection positivity in the crossed channel controls the sum over the spectrum. Together with a
detector sum rule for the fourth angular harmonic, this gives
\begin{equation}
\tau\le0.0178\quad\text{at }\Delta_g=1000,
\label{eq:angular}
\end{equation}
decreasing like $17.8/\Delta_g$ at large gap~\cite{SM}. Besides (i) and (ii), this requires that the detector
identities hold as operator identities (the ladder is checked exactly for spins up to 30 and at spin 40), that the mixed detector
commutator exists and converges in the light-cone limit, as assumed in Ref.~\cite{KKSDZ}, and that $T\times T$ contains no scalar of
dimension at most one. Its $1/\Delta_g$ scaling is far from optimal.

\textit{Locating the string scale}---Both bounds can be read the other way [Fig.~\ref{fig:bounds}(a)]. A large-$c_T$
CFT$_3$ that obeys (ii), and whose stress tensor deviates from Einstein gravity by $\tau$, must contain a single trace of
spin four or more in $T\times T$ with twist below $g_*(\tau)\approx2+17.8/\tau$, by Eq.~\eqref{eq:angular}; the approximation is good to $1\%$ for $\tau\le1$, and the exact $g_*$ is given in the Supplemental Material~\cite{SM}. Under the assumptions of Eq.~\eqref{eq:cubic}, any $\tau>2.9\times10^{-8}$ requires such a
state below twist 1000. A measurement of the three-point function of the stress tensor thus bounds the mass of the lightest
higher-spin state, the string scale of the bulk, from above. Large-$N$ Chern--Simons-matter theories, in which $\tau$ is of
order one and higher-spin currents have twist close to one~\cite{GPY}, satisfy this comfortably.

\textit{How the bounds arise}---Light-transformed stress tensors~\cite{KSDlight} act as energy detectors at
null infinity, and two detectors commute when they are spacelike separated. Integrating the expectation
value of their double commutator against a weight gives a sum rule~\cite{KKSDZ,CLSD}. If the weight decays
fast enough at high energy and fixed momentum transfer, the sum rule converges and its total vanishes. This
holds in every unitary CFT, whose Regge intercept is at most one~\cite{KKSDZ,CaronHuotInversion,PSZ}; the
larger intercept of planar graviton exchange never enters, because we use the sum rule at finite $c_T$ and
expand only its light part in $1/c_T$, as in Refs.~\cite{CHMRS,CLSD}. Expanding in the $T\times T$ OPE
splits the sum rule into the light states (the identity, $T$ and its multi-traces below the gap) and the
heavy ones,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\text{heavy}}(\text{OPE coefficient})^2\times\Omega(\text{state})=-\,\Omega(\text{light}).
\label{eq:sumrule}
\end{equation}
At leading order the light side is fixed by the bulk couplings: graviton exchange, $|\hat g_3|^2$ and, for
the quartic functional, $g_4$. If $\Omega$ is positive on every heavy state, the left side is non-negative, so
$\Omega(\text{light})\le0$; this inequality is the bound. In the flat limit the heavy side becomes the partial-wave
expansion of graviton scattering, and positivity becomes the positivity used in flat space~\cite{CHLPS}. The
difficulty in AdS is that curvature moves the contribution of each heavy state away from its flat value, by
amounts that are not small near the gap or at large impact parameter.

We control these shifts as follows. We use a dual tensor structure for which AdS curvature produces no
helicity mixing at leading Regge order; in a model calculation, the mixing produced by the standard structure
makes the functional negative at large impact parameter. We also choose the weights so that every heavy state
contributes its flat-space value at leading Regge order, to about one part in $10^6$, so that only the smaller
corrections beyond leading order can spoil positivity. For the quartic bound, the $R^4$ contact sits exactly at the convergence border of the sum rule and leaves a finite Regge ``arc'',
which we normalize by matching to a heavy scalar whose exchange produces an $R^4$ contact. On the light side we
derive that no term linear in $t_4$ or $\alpha_T$ survives, because such terms flip helicity and the dual structure
removes helicity flips. A free-field evaluation of the key step confirms this to $10^{-11}$. The cubic bound then follows
from the ratio of the graviton pole to the $|\hat g_3|^2$ term, whose coefficient we compute in closed form.


Light single traces of spin zero and two, which (i) allows, enter the sum rule like heavy states. We sketch why Eq.~\eqref{eq:cubic} still holds when they are present. Their contribution is fixed by their Regge behavior up to an AdS mass shift, and we prove that the cubic functional is positive on them in the flat limit at every mass, so they enter with the favorable sign and the cubic bound also bounds their couplings~\cite{CHLPS}. The quartic functional, by contrast, is negative on scalars lighter than $0.46\Lambda$,
which couple to $TT$ through $\phi W^2$ (cf.\ Ref.~\cite{CMT}); this is the origin of the scalar condition.

\textit{Positivity on every heavy state}---In flat space we certify that the cubic functional is positive. In AdS
we first certify that its leading-Regge part loses less than $0.1\%$ of the flat-space margin on every heavy state at $\Delta_g=1000$. We then show that at $\Delta_g=1000$ the remaining finite-mass corrections do not
overturn this anywhere in the infinite heavy spectrum.

Taking the detector identities behind Eq.~\eqref{eq:angular} as operator identities, we rewrite the action of the functional on any heavy
state exactly as a positive multiple of a finite ``ladder'' sum in each of two helicity
channels, with spectral data of the published weight as coefficients. We cover the heavy spectrum with certificates
in interval or directed-rounding arithmetic (Fig.~\ref{fig:coverage}). Spins up to 1000 are certified one by one,
and high twist by an expansion about the leading-Regge value. For larger spins near the twist edge we use a
localized bound at large impact parameter, an exact resummation at intermediate impact parameter, and a direct
evaluation at small impact parameter. The main obstacle is cancellation. The ladder sum lies between about $10^{-5}$ and $10$, while its individual terms reach $10^{685}$, and the finite-mass correction has no favorable sign. The certificates for large spin near the twist edge were rerun from source on separate hardware with identical output, and the main new lemmas behind them were rederived independently; the spin-by-spin and high-twist certificates were run once~\cite{SM}.

For the quartic functional positivity remains an assumption. With error control it holds on its binding states
near the gap and at masses $1.3$--$3\Lambda$; elsewhere its values are computed in models or interpolated, and no
computed state is negative~\cite{SM}.

\textit{A check with ABJM theory}---In the type-IIA 't~Hooft regime of ABJM theory~\cite{ABJM} the higher-spin
gap is the string scale: supergravity fields have spin at most two, and the lightest single trace of spin four
or more is a string state at the first massive level, and a gap of 1000 corresponds to a 't~Hooft coupling $\lambda\approx3\times10^9$. Assumptions (i) and (ii) therefore hold at leading order.
Through the orders computed, only the stress-tensor multiplet is exchanged in $T\times T$ at tree
level~\cite{CPY,BCP}, so the scalar condition also holds. We sketch both arguments. The flat limit gives
$g_4M^6/8\pi G=2\zeta(3)$~\cite{CHMRSswamp,BCP}, $2.6\%$ of our bound at $\Delta_g=1000$, with an uncertainty of
a few percent from identifying $\Lambda$ with the string mass in AdS units. This is a consistency check, good to about one significant
figure, and not evidence for positivity.

\textit{Conclusions}---The cubic and quartic graviton couplings of a holographic CFT$_3$ with a large gap in the
single traces of $T\times T$ are, in units of the gap, at most of order a hundred (the quartic one under the conditions stated above), as expected when the gap sets
the size of higher-derivative terms. The cubic bound is about ten times weaker in $|\hat g_3|$ than the flat-space
optimum transplanted to AdS~\cite{CHLPS}, which uses sum rules not available here. The quartic bound is
dominated by an infrared logarithm. Relative to the parametric statements of Refs.~\cite{KKSDZ,BHM,AKT} and the
flat-space and higher-dimensional precedents~\cite{CHLPS,CHLPSpw}, we give explicit constants derived from $\langle TTTT\rangle$, a computer-assisted proof that a dispersive functional is positive on the entire heavy spectrum of AdS at
finite gap, a bound on the parity-odd coupling $\alpha_T$, an upper bound on the string scale in terms of the measured deviation, and, assuming positivity of the quartic functional, an
AdS bound on a graviton $R^4$ coefficient, which localization determines in ABJ(M) theory~\cite{BCP,BCJP}. Because the constants are explicit, the bounds can be compared directly with string theory, as the ABJM check
shows, and used as inputs in constraints on gravitational effective theories. The method should carry over to other gaps, where positivity has to be established anew, and to higher dimensions; AdS$_5$/CFT$_4$, where $\mathcal N=4$ super-Yang--Mills theory supplies exact checks, is the natural
next case.
