# DERIVATIONS

Working calculations behind PROOFS.md. Items marked *heuristic* are not part of any proof.

## 1. Liouville measure and the Crofton constant

Upper half-plane, geodesic = semicircle with Euclidean centre `c`, radius `ρ`, endpoints `a = c − ρ`,
`b = c + ρ`. Invariant measure `∝ da db/(b − a)² = 2 dc dρ/(4ρ²)`. Take `dm = dc dρ/(2ρ²)`.

Geodesics meeting the vertical segment `{x = 0, y_1 < y < y_2}`: `|c| < ρ` and the height at `x = 0`,
`h = sqrt(ρ² − c²)`, lies in `(y_1, y_2)`. With `c = ρ sin φ`, `h = ρ cos φ`, `dc = ρ cos φ dφ`:

`m = ½ ∫_{−π/2}^{π/2} cos φ dφ ∫_{y_1/cos φ}^{y_2/cos φ} dρ/ρ = ½ · 2 · log(y_2/y_1) = log(y_2/y_1)`,

the hyperbolic length of the segment. So the Crofton constant is 1 with this normalisation.

## 2. The universal end deficit `κ = 1 − log 2`

Geodesics that cross the ray `{x = 0}` below height `Y` and also enter the horoball `{y > Y}`:
`ρ > Y` and `ρ cos φ < Y`. Measure:

`½ ∫ cos φ dφ ∫_{Y}^{Y/cos φ} dρ/ρ = ∫_0^{π/2} cos φ · log(1/cos φ) dφ`.

With `s = sin φ`: `∫_0^1 −½ log(1 − s²) ds = −½[∫_0^1 log(1−s) ds + ∫_0^1 log(1+s) ds] = −½[−1 + (2 log 2 − 1)] = 1 − log 2 ≈ 0.30685`.

It is independent of `Y` (dilation invariance), which is why the deficit per arc-end is universal. Numerical
confirmation by the referee: 0.3068528194; end-to-end check of Lemma B in `H²` with two marked points
converges to `2κ = 0.61370564`.

## 3. Geodesics of `AdS_3 = SL(2,R)`

Metric `ds² = −det(dx)`; for `x ∈ SL(2,R)`, tangent vectors satisfy `tr(x^{-1} dx) = 0`.

* `det(s·ab^T + t·cd^T) = st·[(a_1c_2 − a_2c_1)(b_1d_2 − b_2d_1)] = st·ω(a,c)ω(b,d)`.
  (A rank-one matrix has zero determinant, and the determinant is a quadratic form whose polarisation on two
  rank-one matrices is the stated product.)
* Hence `x(t) = (e^t p + e^{−t} q)/√N`, `N = ω(a,c)ω(b,d) > 0`, lies in `SL(2,R)`, `x'' = x`, `−det(x') = 1`:
  a unit-speed spacelike geodesic between the boundary points `q` (`t → −∞`) and `p` (`t → +∞`).
* Truncating where either coefficient exceeds `1/ε` leaves `|t| ≤ log(√N/ε)`: length `log N + 2 log(1/ε)`.
* Closed geodesics: for `(g_L, g_R) = (diag(e^{a/2}, e^{−a/2}), diag(e^{b/2}, e^{−b/2}))`,
  `x ↦ g_L x g_R^{-1}` scales `(x_11, x_12, x_21, x_22)` by `(e^{(a−b)/2}, e^{(a+b)/2}, e^{−(a+b)/2}, e^{(b−a)/2})`.
  Invariant geodesics: `x_11 = x_22 = 0` (translation `(a+b)/2`) and `x_12 = x_21 = 0` (translation
  `(a−b)/2`). For BTZ, `a = 2π(r_+ − r_−)`, `b = 2π(r_+ + r_−)` give `2πr_+` and `2πr_−`.

So spacetime length = half the sum of two hyperbolic lengths, with one group element acting on both sides:
this is the origin of "the same homotopy class in `X_L` and `X_R`".

## 4. Why cut-and-paste is not metric-independent (the failed Approach 2)

Three curves `a, b, c` through a small disc forming a triangle `T`. Label the seven regions by which side of
each curve they are on, relative to `T = 000`: edge-adjacent regions `100, 010, 001`, vertex-adjacent
`110, 011, 101`. Around the disc boundary the six outer regions appear in the order
`100, 110, 010, 011, 001, 101`. After a triangle move the central region is `111`, edge-adjacent to
`110, 011, 101`.

Let `m` be the indicator of `U_r` on bit strings and suppose `m(000) = m(100) = m(010) = m(001) = 1`,
`m(110) = m(011) = m(101) = m(111) = 0`.

* Before: `U_r ∩ disc` is the connected star `T ∪ {100, 010, 001}`; its boundary is three arcs, each running
  around one of the weight-2 wedges.
* After: `U_r ∩ disc` is three disjoint wedges; its boundary is three arcs, each running around one of the
  weight-1 wedges.

The six boundary points are paired differently, so the global curves can differ in homotopy class. A
contraction map constrains `Σ_r β_r |f(x)_r − f(x')_r|`, not individual coordinates, so such an `m` can occur.

## 5. Hypothesis H from maximin *(heuristic; at the rigour of Wall's theorems)*

Claim: HRT(A) = `min_{c ∈ 𝒞(A)} L(c)`.

(≥) By Wall (arXiv:1211.3494) the maximin surface exists, is extremal, and is the minimal extremal surface.
In vacuum 2+1 an extremal surface is a union of geodesics; lying on a Cauchy slice and homologous to `A`
there, it is the realisation of a class in `𝒞(A)`. So HRT(A) is `L(c)` for some class.

(≤) Fix any class `c` and any Cauchy slice `Σ`. For each component geodesic `g_i` of `c`, pass to the cover
of `M` in which `g_i` is embedded (cyclic cover for a closed component), and let `Σ̂` be the lift of `Σ`. The
boundary of the domain of dependence of one side of `g_i` is generated by null geodesics orthogonal to `g_i`;
in vacuum `AdS_3` their expansion vanishes identically (Raychaudhuri with `θ = 0` initially and no shear in
2+1), so lengths are preserved along generators until they leave the congruence. Its intersection with `Σ̂`
is therefore a curve in the class of `g_i` of length `≤ L(g_i)`. Projecting to `Σ` gives curves in the classes
of the components of `c`, whose union is mod-2 homologous to `A` on `Σ`. So the minimal curve on `Σ`
homologous to `A` has length `≤ L(c)`; maximising over `Σ`, maximin `≤ L(c)`.

Not proved: existence of maximin surfaces in these spacetimes; that the null congruence meets every Cauchy
slice in a full curve of the right class. This is why H is listed as an assumption.

## 6. Finite families of test curves are graphs *(now Lemma G in PROOFS.md)*

For `μ = Σ w_k τ_k`, draw the `τ_k` in minimal position. A cut `c` with region `W` cuts exactly the segments of
the `τ_k` that have one end in `W`. Conversely a set `S` of crossing points, thickened, is a region whose
boundary crosses each segment leaving `S` once. So `min_c i(μ,c)` is the min-cut of the graph whose vertices
are crossing points and ideal ends and whose edges are segments. This gives the whole cone, not only
contraction-map inequalities, and is a second proof of Theorem 1 for finite `μ` that does not use Lemma A.
